# 🌊 Abyss Atlas — Deep Ocean Exploration

### Developed by Stephany Gonçalves da Silva

**Abyss Atlas** is an interactive deep-ocean exploration project focused on the least known environments on Earth.

The project combines scientific information, visual storytelling and front-end development to create an immersive experience about ocean depth, the Mariana Trench, deep-sea biodiversity, scientific discoveries and exploration technologies.

🌊 **Live Project:**  
https://silva-stephany.github.io/abyss-atlas/

---

## 🌌 About the Project

Abyss Atlas was created from a fascination with oceanography, deep-sea environments and the idea that large parts of our own planet still remain difficult to observe and fully understand.

The project explores the ocean as a scientific frontier.

Rather than presenting information as a traditional static website, Abyss Atlas uses depth progression, visual transitions, interactive layouts and scientific storytelling to guide users from the surface of the ocean to some of its most extreme environments.

---

## 💙 Why This Project Matters to Me

Oceanography has always been one of the subjects that fascinates me most.

I have always been especially interested in the Mariana Trench, extreme marine environments and the possibility that organisms, ecosystems and biological behaviors may still remain poorly understood or undiscovered.

That curiosity became the starting point for Abyss Atlas.

This project allowed me to connect a personal interest in ocean exploration with my development in technology, front-end development and scientific visualization.

## 🌊 Main Experience

Abyss Atlas is structured as a multi-page scientific exploration experience.

### 🌐 Overview

The homepage introduces the project and presents key information about deep-ocean exploration.

It includes:

- Seafloor mapping context
- Visual observation of the deep ocean
- Challenger Deep
- Deep-ocean depth zones
- A visual introduction to the descent from the surface into deeper waters

---

## ↓ Depth Explorer

The **Depth Explorer** is one of the central experiences of the project.

It follows the ocean vertically through different depth zones:

- Sunlight Zone
- Twilight Zone
- Midnight Zone
- Abyssal Zone
- Hadal Zone
- Challenger Deep

As the user moves deeper through the page, the visual environment gradually becomes darker.

This transition was designed to represent the decreasing availability of sunlight and the increasingly extreme conditions found in deeper ocean environments.

---

## ▼ Mariana Trench

The **Mariana Trench** page explores one of the deepest known regions of Earth's ocean.

The page presents:

- Challenger Deep
- Approximate maximum depth
- Geographic context
- Hadal environments
- Subduction geology
- Extreme pressure
- Comparison with Mount Everest
- Historical exploration milestones

The visual design emphasizes scale and depth to help communicate how extreme this environment really is.

---

## ✦ Deep-Sea Species

The **Species** page focuses on biological adaptations found in deep-ocean environments.

Topics include:

- Bioluminescence
- Specialized vision
- Extreme pressure tolerance
- Ultra-dark camouflage
- Low-light environments
- Deep-sea organisms
- Hidden biodiversity
- Environmental DNA

The goal is not simply to create a catalog of animals, but to explain how life adapts to conditions that are dramatically different from those at the surface.

---

## 🧬 Discoveries

The **Discoveries** page explores how scientific knowledge of the deep ocean continues to evolve.

The project highlights different forms of discovery, including:

- New species
- New depth records
- Geographic range extensions
- Previously undocumented behaviors
- New habitats
- Genetic evidence

It also presents a simplified discovery workflow:

**Explore → Observe → Sample → Analyze → Discover**

This section reflects the idea that scientific discovery often continues long after an expedition ends.

---

## 🤿 Expeditions

The **Expeditions** page focuses on the technologies and processes that make deep-ocean exploration possible.

The project introduces:

- Remotely Operated Vehicles — ROVs
- Autonomous Underwater Vehicles — AUVs
- Multibeam sonar
- Telepresence
- Research vessels
- Mapping systems
- Scientific sensors

It also explains how exploration missions typically move through stages such as:

**Mapping → Target Selection → Dive → Analysis → Data Archiving**

## 🛠️ Technologies

Abyss Atlas was built with:

- HTML5
- CSS3
- Git
- GitHub
- GitHub Pages

The project was developed as a multi-page website without a front-end framework.

The visual experience is created primarily through HTML structure, CSS gradients, responsive layouts, transparency effects and depth-based color transitions.

---

## 🎨 Visual Design

The visual identity of Abyss Atlas was inspired by deep-ocean environments.

The design uses:

- Ocean blue gradients
- Deep navy backgrounds
- Cyan accents
- Bioluminescent glow effects
- Glassmorphism-inspired cards
- Scientific interface elements
- Depth-based color transitions
- Subtle sonar and radar-inspired graphics
- Responsive layouts

One of the main design decisions was to make the website progressively darker as the user explores deeper ocean environments.

This creates a visual relationship between the interface and the scientific subject being presented.

## 💡 Concept

The central idea behind Abyss Atlas can be summarized by one question:

**How much do we really know about the deepest environments on our own planet?**

The project explores that question through depth, biodiversity, exploration and scientific discovery.

Its visual narrative begins closer to the ocean surface and gradually descends into darker and more extreme environments.
