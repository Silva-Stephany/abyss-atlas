BEYOND NEW SPECIES

Discovery has many forms

Ocean exploration does not only discover species that are new to science. A single expedition can reveal entirely different types of biological information.

✦

New Species

Organisms may be collected and later identified as species previously unknown to science.

↔

Range Extensions

A known species may be observed much farther from its previously documented geographic range.

↓

Depth Records

Familiar organisms can appear at depths where they had never previously been recorded.

👁

New Behavior

Direct observation can reveal feeding, reproduction, interactions and other behaviors rarely seen before.

≋

New Habitats

Exploration can reveal coral gardens, sponge communities, vents and other previously undocumented ecosystems.

🧬

Genetic Evidence

DNA found in seawater can indicate the presence of organisms that cameras may never encounter.

ENVIRONMENTAL DNA

Finding life without seeing it

Organisms constantly leave traces of genetic material in their environment. Scientists can collect seawater and analyze this environmental DNA — eDNA — for evidence of nearby life.

This creates another window into deep-ocean biodiversity, especially where direct observation is difficult.

2026

NOAA Ocean Exploration released its first eDNA data from expeditions aboard NOAA Ship Okeanos Explorer.
